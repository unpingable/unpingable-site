Automated work you can plan, bound, and review

Constellation helps you turn an intention into a plan, control which changes are allowed, and inspect what actually happened. Planning work, granting permission, executing it, and checking the result remain separate jobs.

Operations is the first proving ground, not the limit of the project. The ECAD/design-flow work explores how to design, decompose and review work upstream; it remains pre-alpha.

A small example

Suppose you want to test a disposable local HTTP service with two caches. You describe the components, checks, and teardown in a plan. Below is the intended journey; the connected run has not yet been verified in the current public tutorial kit.

  1. Maude prepares the plan. You review its meaning and lock the exact revision. This does not run or authorize anything.
  2. Nightshift records current conditions and the proposal. Evidence from an earlier run is not automatically current evidence.
  3. Constellation AG decides whether this run may proceed. Permission applies only to this exact work and can be used once.
  4. Docket keeps custody of the attempt. It records the execution outcome without deciding that the work is acceptable.
  5. A fresh observation checks the result. Teardown is separate work with its own permission, not an automatic consequence of success.
  6. Phosphor lets you inspect the record. You can read what was requested, allowed, attempted, observed, and settled. Phosphor does not change the run.
What is exercisable today: Maude's deterministic, credential-free Plan Core path can author, check, compare, and lock a draft; Phosphor's deterministic local demo can render production-engine-derived read models at its loopback route. Neither path executes the cache plan. What is not yet verified in the current public kit: the connected Docker/runtime journey, the new NQ integration, Pulse public distribution, and public-only installation.

What the family separates

Execution outcome says whether the work succeeded, failed, or remains uncertain according to the available evidence. Settlement records the attempt's result in the owning runtime. A settled record does not mean the work succeeded, and it does not grant permission for another run.

Attention means a known condition needs review. Unknown means the available observation cannot establish the answer. Unknown is not a softer word for failure.

A concrete read-only example: immediately after Docket accepts an attempt, custody exists but its execution outcome is still unknown. Later, an executor-backed settlement may record success or failure. Nightshift derives attention/currentness separately; Docket acceptance or settlement alone does not establish that value. Phosphor displays these owner records together without deriving one from another or granting permission to retry.
