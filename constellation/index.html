Governance and execution custody for automated work

Constellation separates observation, proposal, judgment, one-use authorization, execution custody, and reconciliation. Automated work can produce a real effect without giving one component ambient authority over the whole process.

Try what shipped

Follow one real reviewed action

In the alpha.6 qualification, a user asked for one 38-byte local file. The exact plan was independently reviewed and explicitly accepted, AG authorized it once, Docket kept custody of the attempt, an authority-neutral executor created it once, and the result settled successfully.

A clean newcomer check then cloned the immutable public release anonymously and reproduced its evidence and refusal checks without contacting the provider, granting authority, or repeating the effect.

Why use it?

An agent or workflow can propose work without authorizing it. A provider response can inform a review without accepting a plan. Permission can name one exact occurrence without becoming an open-ended credential. Docket can record an uncertain attempt and reconcile that same attempt instead of blindly repeating it.

The governing cycle is deliberately finite: effect → fresh observation → fresh judgment → fresh authority. A successful or settled run does not authorize the next one.

What happened in alpha.6?

  1. Observe. NQ admitted bounded evidence; Pulse supported its currentness.
  2. Propose. Maude compiled the exact plan; Nightshift admitted the occurrence.
  3. Review and accept. Switchyard retained one bounded independent review, followed by explicit acceptance of the exact candidate.
  4. Authorize once. AG judged and spent authority for that exact work.
  5. Execute under custody. Docket resolved one bounded local permission snapshot and dispatched the authority-neutral executor once.
  6. Settle and inspect. Docket reconciled a successful settlement; a later read observed the exact file bytes.
  7. Stop. The workflow ended at settled_observation_required. Nothing in the release grants continuation authority.

Which components do I need?

The released reviewed-local-copy/v1 composition requires Maude, NQ, Pulse, Nightshift/Foreman, Switchyard Runtime, Constellation AG, and Constellation Docket. Phosphor is the optional read-only inspection surface. Other use cases need smaller or different profiles; Constellation is not an all-or-nothing platform.

What remains alpha or unqualified?

  • Alpha.6 qualifies one named Linux local-copy composition, not arbitrary executors, providers, or production deployments.
  • Live Slack, Discord, and PagerDuty delivery—and human acknowledgment—were not verified. Deterministic adapters and local inbox delivery are narrower evidence.
  • Cross-version restore, migration, and rollback remain unqualified.
  • Operational burn-in is a separate, bounded post-release phase; it does not change the immutable alpha.6 result.
  • ATProto received an exact shared handback. Its application binding, production deployment, migration, notifications, rollback, and legacy-NQ retirement remain ATProto-owned work.
  • ECAD/design-flow remains pre-alpha. Operations is the first proving ground, not the permanent boundary of the project.
