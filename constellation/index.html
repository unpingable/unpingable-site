Constellation · experimental

Systems forget how they know.

A command that exited zero, a green check, an agent that says “done”: each is evidence from one moment and one participant. None of them proves that the intended state is true now.

A CI check goes green at 09:00. At 11:00 a deploy relies on that green. Nothing in between asked whether it still held. The check was honest; the deploy treated a two-hour-old observation as a current fact.

AI coding agents make this sharper. When an agent reports that a task is done, that is a report from the worker, not evidence that the intended change exists.

What this is

Constellation is an experimental system for separating work from the evidence, authority and acceptance used to decide whether that work succeeded. A worker, whether a script or an AI agent, may propose and perform work. Separate programs decide whether that exact work may run, keep a record of each attempt, observe the result independently, and report “unknown” when the evidence does not settle the question. The programs remain independently useful, and combinations of them are named, pinned and tested one at a time.

Related work

Recent work independently studies the same basic separation inside a single agent runtime: agents act, but trusted evidence determines authoritative completion. Constellation explores the harder distributed case, where the observer, authority, executor and evidence sources may be independently owned and may fail separately.

See Li et al., “Who Holds the Pen? Let Specifications, Not Agents, Sign Off”, arXiv:2609.29921 (2026). This is independent convergence and evidence for the discipline, not validation of Constellation’s specific architecture. A longer literature note compares the two.

From this project: “Δt-Constrained Inference: A General Model of Temporal Coherence in Hierarchical Systems” and “Cybernetic Fault Domains: When Commitment Outruns Verification”. The full catalogue is on the research page, and the questions behind the software are on research directions.

Status and limits

Constellation is experimental, and real composed workloads run on it. The 0.1.0-alpha.6 release records one reviewed, explicitly accepted file change that was authorized once, executed once and then checked by a separate read; anyone can re-verify that evidence from public source. On 2026-09-25 a packaged release candidate installed cleanly from its artifacts in a fresh virtual machine and passed its end-to-end and refusal checks. Those packages are not public yet, and nothing has been released beyond alpha.6 and a package for one of its components. It is not production-ready.
